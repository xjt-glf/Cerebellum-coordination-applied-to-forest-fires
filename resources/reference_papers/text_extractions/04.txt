Abstract—This study proposes a novel Agentic Retrieval-
augmented generation with Mamba-Attention Integrated Trans-
former (ARMAIT) framework for multi-Uncrewed Aerial Vehicle
(UA V) trajectory optimization. The framework is built upon
Large Language Models (LLMs), incorporating Retrieval-
Augmented Generation (RAG) empowered by Agentic AI and
integrated with a UA V-speciﬁc knowledge base. Through the
Agentic RAG, the LLM autonomously interprets high-level task
requirements and identiﬁes the key components necessary for
trajectory optimization, including model inputs and outputs,
network architecture, reward functions, and task constraints.
To support efﬁcient modeling across different system scales,
we introduce the Mamba-Attention Integrated Transformer
(MAIT), a hybrid neural architecture that combines the long-
range dependency modeling capability of attention mechanisms
with the efﬁcient temporal dynamic representation of Mamba.
Furthermore, a Trajectory-Group Relative Policy Optimization
(T-GRPO) method is proposed to achieve uniﬁed policy gradient
optimization in both discrete and continuous trajectory spaces
for MAIT training. Extensive experimental results validate the
feasibility and effectiveness of the proposed ARMAIT framework.
Index Terms —Agentic AI, large language model, Mamba,
uncrewed aerial vehicle, GRPO, Agentic RAG.

Moreover, Retrieval-Augmented Generation (RAG) pro-

To advance the autonomous decision-making and tra-
jectory optimization capabilities of multi-UA V systems in
LAENets, this paper explores the integration of the Agentic
Artiﬁcial Intelligence (AI) paradigm. By combining Large
Language Models (LLMs), trajectory planning models, and
Reinforcement Learning (RL) algorithms, we construct an
Agentic RAG with Mamba-Attention Integrated Transformer
(ARMAIT) framework that uniﬁes task comprehension,
knowledge retrieval, model formulation and training, and
trajectory generation. The main contributions of this study are
summarized as follows:
1) Agentic RAG for Task Modeling: We design an Agentic
AI framework that integrates LLMs with a UA V-speciﬁc
knowledge base. Leveraging an Agentic RAG mechanism,
the LLM is empowered to autonomously interpret user intent
from natural language task instructions, retrieve relevant UA V
knowledge, and automatically generate the required model
components, including input representations (e.g., state space),
output objectives (e.g., action space), model architecture (e.g.,
the ratio of Attention to Mamba layers), reward function
deﬁnitions, and task constraints. This mechanism establishes a
closed-loop process from language understanding to task mod-
eling, signiﬁcantly enhancing the adaptability and generality
of multi-UA V trajectory planning tasks.
2) MAIT for Trajectory Optimization:We propose a Mamba-
Attention Integrated Transformer (MAIT) model, which
combines the spatial global dependency modeling strength of
multi-head attention with the efﬁcient long-sequence mod-
eling capability of the Mamba architecture. MAIT supports
parallel interactions among UA Vs while capturing temporal
dynamics essential for trajectory planning. The UA V system
states are encoded into structured input sequences, and the
model architecture adapts the attention-to-Mamba layer ratio
automatically, favoring attention layers for smaller-scale UA V
swarms and Mamba layers for larger-scale ones. This design
ensures strong generalization and high decision-making efﬁ-
ciency.
3) T-GRPO for Model Training: We present Trajectory-
Group Relative Policy Optimization (T-GRPO), a policy-
gradient Reinforcement Learning (RL) algorithm tailored for
multi-UA V trajectory generation. Building upon the original
GRPO method, T-GRPO extends its applicability to both
discrete (e.g., stop point sequences) and continuous (e.g., ﬂight
angles and distances) action spaces. The algorithm deﬁnes
state and action spaces for UA V swarms as well as a global
reward function. It introduces a group-based relative advantage
function and policy divergence constraints to optimize the
policy. This enables the stable training of the MAIT model for
generating globally coordinated multi-UA V trajectories across
diverse task scenarios.

II. R ELATED WORK
A. Reinforcement Learning–Enhanced UAV Systems
Wang et al. proposed a Deep Reinforcement Learn-
ing (DRL)-based trajectory control algorithm for UA V-
assisted mobile edge computing, aiming to minimize system
energy consumption through dynamic path adaptation [10].
Dong et al. utilized an Actor–Critic (AC)-based method to
train an attention-based UA V trajectory optimizer [11]. Xue
et al. introduced a data-driven trajectory imitation method
grounded in inverse reinforcement learning, employing static
output feedback control to enhance stability and control accu-
racy [12]. Additionally, Liu and Huang developed an adaptive
and robust DDPG framework for aerial manipulators, focusing
on quadrotor tracking control while mitigating the impact of
robotic-arm motion on ﬂight dynamics [13].
B. Transformer-Enhanced UAV Systems
In multi-UA V area coverage, Chen et al. employed a Trans-
former encoder within their T-MARL algorithm to adapt to
variable numbers of UA Vs and users [14]. Zhu et al. utilized
a Transformer encoder-decoder architecture to translate the
entire UA V-IoT system state into an optimized visiting order,
achieving good generalization and computational efﬁciency
[15]. Feng et al. embedded a lightweight Transformer into the
critic network of each UA V base station in their EDT-MARL
framework [16]. Furthermore, Wang et al. used a Vision Trans-
former (ViT)-based model for robust visual feature learning
in the main tracker, complemented by a separate Transformer-
based module for predicting target motion trajectories from
sequential data [17].
C. Mamba-Enhanced UAV Systems
Xiao et al. proposed a temporally audio-driven Mamba
framework that fuses spectral and temporal information to
achieve accurate drone trajectory estimation and classiﬁcation
under complex acoustic conditions [18]. Tao et al. introduced
FDMamba-Net, which incorporates feature decoupling along
with multi-layer Mamba blocks, enabling efﬁcient and highly
accurate ﬂight trajectory prediction with signiﬁcantly reduced

III. ARMAIT F RAMEWORK
The ARMAIT framework comprises three key components:
an Agentic RAG-based task analysis module, the MAIT tra-
jectory generator, and the T-GRPO optimizer. The Agentic
RAG module couples an LLM with a UA V-domain knowledge
base to parse natural-language task descriptions, infer the
required problem formulation, and automatically instantiate
the model architecture and data interfaces for MAIT and
T-GRPO. MAIT integrates attention-based global representa-
tion with Mamba’s efﬁcient sequence modeling to produce
coordinated trajectories for multiple UA Vs. T-GRPO jointly
optimizes policies over discrete and continuous action spaces
and trains MAIT to generate trajectories that satisfy multiple
constraints. Together, these modules form a closed-loop, end-
to-end pipeline that bridges natural-language understanding
and multi-UA V trajectory planning, as illustrated in Fig. 1.
A. Agentic RAG Task Analysis Module
To effectively analyze diverse UA V path-planning tasks,
LLMs have been increasingly adopted for their strong natural-
language understanding and reasoning capabilities. However,
existing LLM-based task analysis often lacks sufﬁcient UA V-
domain knowledge, which can lead to formulation errors and
hallucinated content, ultimately undermining the generation
of task-aligned planning solutions in real-world deployments.
RAG alleviates these issues by introducing semantic retrieval
prior to generation, enriching the query with contextual evi-
dence and thereby reducing hallucinations and knowledge
staleness [24]. Nevertheless, when applied to UA V task anal-
ysis, conventional RAG still suffers from two key limitations:
(1) The user query cannot dynamically adapt to the evolving
task context of UA Vs, resulting in inconsistencies between
the retrieved content and the generation objective; (2) The
lack of procedural control prevents dynamic adjustment of
the retrieval strategy or reﬁnement of the query expression
based on intermediate outputs, thereby inhibiting autonomous
reﬂection and self-correction.
To overcome the limitations of static modeling and single-
round inference in traditional RAG systems, this study
introduces a novel Agentic RAG architecture for UA V task
modeling and reasoning. This system integrates the core prin-
ciples of Agentic AI, which endow LLMs with autonomous
perception, reasoning, and proactive planning capabilities. By
enabling dynamic decision-making and self-directed retrieval
for complex UA V tasks, Agentic RAG establishes a closed-
loop task-solving pipeline with autonomous reasoning chains
for trajectory planning. The following outlines the operational

a target area.”

models, ﬂight altitude, or battery constraints. It then prompts

prior knowledge. For example, the Agent may automatically
ask: “Please provide the geographical distribution of IoT
devices, ﬂight distance limitations, and the maximum payload
capacity of each UA V for improved modeling accuracy.”
4) Context Retrieval: The clariﬁed query is submitted to a
retrieval engine, which includes a vector database constructed
from UA V trajectory planning literature, a tool library com-
posed of various UA V-related models (such as UA V dynamics
models and wireless channel models), an algorithm library
containing diverse UA V-related algorithms (such as convex
optimization algorithms and genetic algorithms), and API
interfaces with direct access to the Internet.
5) Context Integration and Generation: The Agent inte-
grates the retrieved context with the user’s original intent for
downstream modeling. For instance, it may issue a prompt
such as: “Based on the retrieved knowledge, please generate
a draft design of a multi-UA V trajectory optimization system.
Include the deﬁnition of state space, action space, constraint
condition, reward function, and recommend a suitable MAIT
architecture and T-GRPO method.” This then triggers the LLM
to perform generation and reasoning, resulting in an initial
output, such as:
• State space: Position, battery level, task load, etc.
• Action space: Discrete motion primitives.
• Reward function : The total UA V energy consumption,
including ﬂight energy, hovering energy, and wireless
charging energy.
• Model architecture: A Transformer with 10 encoder lay-
ers, comprising 50% Mamba and 50% attention layers.
• Training method: Discrete trajectory training with 8 inde-
pendent rollouts.
• Task constraints: The total energy consumption must not
exceed the UA V’s maximum energy capacity.
6) Self-Veriﬁcation and Evaluation: The Agent semanti-
cally veriﬁes the generated output by checking whether all
essential parameters are included and whether the proposed
design satisﬁes task constraints. For example, the Agent may
identify the absence of resource constraints.

B. Mamba-Attention Integrated Transformer
To enhance both the modeling capacity and computational
efﬁciency of multi-UA V trajectory optimization, we develop
a hybrid framework that integrates Mamba with Transformer
self-attention. In this design, the attention module captures
global dependencies and interaction patterns, whereas the
Mamba module models mobility continuity and the temporal
evolution of resource constraints, reducing the time and mem-
ory complexity of long-sequence processing from quadratic
to linear in sequence length. This complementary integration
enables faster and more accurate trajectory-planning decisions
and improves generalization and robustness in highly dynamic
environments.
The MAIT model is composed of an embedding layer, atten-
tion layers, Mamba layers, and a trajectory output layer. Within
the attention and Mamba layers, there are also Multi-Layer
Perceptron (MLP) sublayers and Root Mean Square Normal-
ization (RMSNorm) sublayers. The MLP sublayer enhances
the representation capacity through linear transformations,
while the RMSNorm sublayer normalizes the data distribution
and stabilizes the training dynamics [26]. The structure of the
MAIT model is illustrated in Fig. 2. The working process of
the MAIT model can be described as follows:
1) Embedding Layer: In the MAIT model, the efﬁcient
representation of input states is critical for capturing spa-
tiotemporal dependencies and modeling collaborative behav-
iors among multiple UA Vs. Unlike the embedding layer in

Mamba-2 layers capture contextual information via selective
state propagation, removing the need for the explicit multi-
head design commonly used in Transformers. To evaluate
MAIT’s scalability in multi-UA V swarm settings, we analyze
its computational complexity. The multi-head self-attention in
the attention branch has quadratic complexity, O(N 2d), so
both computation and memory costs grow rapidly with the
number of UA Vs N. In contrast, the Mamba branch adopts
a selective state-space model with linear complexity, O(Nd),
enabling efﬁcient long-sequence processing without forming
attention matrices. MAIT further adapts the ratio of attention
to Mamba layers to the swarm size: attention layers dominate
in small-to-medium swarms to strengthen global coordination,
whereas Mamba layers dominate in large-scale scenarios to
maintain efﬁcient inference. As a result, MAIT achieves strong
scalability across UA V networks of varying sizes.
4) Trajectory Output Layer: In UA V trajectory optimiza-
tion, the trajectory output layer acts as the terminal module
of the Transformer, decoding the high-dimensional temporal
representations produced by MAIT into executable control
signals and thus bridging the model representation space and
the real ﬂight control space. Designing a structurally clear,
functionally complete, and task-generalizable output mech-
anism that supports both discrete and continuous planning
modes is therefore essential for improving adaptability and
generalization across diverse UA V scenarios.

To systematically evaluate the quality of task formula-
tions generated by different RAG systems, we design an
assessment scheme consisting of four key metrics. (1) State
Correctness: This metric examines whether the generated state
space includes all essential variables and whether each vari-
able is assigned an appropriate type. (2) Action Correctness:
This metric evaluates whether the constructed action space
contains all required elements and whether their types are cor-
rectly speciﬁed. (3) Constraint Coverage: This metric assesses
whether the system correctly identiﬁes and incorporates the
primary task constraints. (4) Reward Consistency: This metric
measures the degree to which the designed reward function
aligns with the underlying task objectives. Model hallucination
is used to assess whether the generated content introduces
ﬁctitious variables, incorrect constraints, or invalid reward
deﬁnitions that are inconsistent with the knowledge base or
the underlying system model. Accordingly, we deﬁne an Anti-
Hallucination Rate to evaluate the impact of hallucination on
the ﬁnal task formulation. This metric measures the proportion
of task elements that remain factually correct, constraint-
compliant, and semantically valid, with each of the four
evaluation dimensions contributing 25% to the ﬁnal score. A
higher Anti-Hallucination Rate indicates greater reliability and
correctness in the generated task modeling results.

VII. C ONCLUSION
This paper proposed the ARMAIT framework, which inte-
grates Agentic RAG for autonomous task understanding, the
MAIT model for efﬁcient trajectory feature modeling, and
T-GRPO for stable RL optimization across mixed action
spaces. ARMAIT enables end-to-end reasoning and trajectory
generation for multi-UA V systems. Experimental results show
that ARMAIT achieves superior decision-making accuracy and
faster convergence than existing approaches and demonstrates
strong generalization across multiple case studies. However,
the current framework still relies on global system information
and focuses only on UA V trajectory optimization, without
jointly considering resource scheduling. In the future, we will
extend ARMAIT to decentralized and partially observable set-
tings and explore joint optimization of trajectory and resource
allocation to enhance overall system performance.
